Kayyan

The world within.

كيان (Kayyan) is an Arabic word that means entity, existence: the whole of who someone is.

About the game

Kayyan is a two-player co-op puzzle game set inside the world of consciousness, where one person is split into two: Kay and Ayan.

The name carries the idea. Kay and Ayan are two halves of one being, and together they make Kayyan.

One person, two halves

Kay and Ayan are the same person, split down the middle by a crack in a mirror. Neither can fix the crack alone. They have to merge into one, so that Kayyan can live his life with purpose and chase the question that waits at the end:

"What is the meaning of life?"

Between two worlds

Our take on the jam theme is that the two worlds are not places you travel between. They are two halves of one mind, living side by side.

The crack between them is the screen divider. It glows brighter every time the two of you help each other, and it lets more light through in every level.

Both worlds are on screen at the same time, one player in each. What you do in one world changes the other. Ripples, rivers and roots carry things across, and neither side finishes alone:

You do this……and this happens in the other world
Dig up a seed in the desert and send it across the riverA root grows in the forest
Wake a river in the forestThe sand in the desert turns soft enough to dig

How it plays

  • Split-screen co-op for two players. Each player has their own half of the screen and their own camera: one plays Kay, the other plays Ayan.
  • Three puzzles, each one earning a Mirror Fragment. Each fragment ends with a short, quiet moment where a flower blooms and a few lines are written out, one letter at a time.
  • Simple controls: move, look, jump, interact, and a single hold action (dig, grow, join) that most puzzles ask both players to use together.
  • No fail state. Fall in a hole and you are put back on your feet. The game wants you to think, not hurry.
  • A soft light beam in each world points you at your next target.

The story is about recognising pain, accepting the past, and finally becoming whole again. We kept it gentle on purpose.
